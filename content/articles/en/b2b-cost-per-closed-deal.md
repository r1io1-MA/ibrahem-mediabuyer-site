In B2B campaigns, the number the ads dashboard shows is **cost per lead**: what you paid for each person who filled the form. It is a comfortable number because it is easy to push down, but on its own it says nothing about sales.

## Why cost per lead misleads you

Cutting cost per lead is easy: a shorter form, a wider audience, a more tempting offer. The result is often less serious leads who do not answer the phone or cannot make the buying decision. The dashboard number goes down while what you really pay for each deal goes up.

## The formula that matters

**Cost per closed deal** = ad spend ÷ closed deals

Or: **cost per lead ÷ close rate**

### A real example

In a <a href="/cases/b2b-snapchat-leads/">B2B Snapchat case study</a>, cost per lead was $1.73 and 70% of leads closed in the CRM. Cost per closed deal was only about $2.47, and the campaign brought SAR 260K in revenue from $1.1K in spend.

With a 5% close rate, the same cost per lead would mean about $35 per deal. Same dashboard number, completely different outcome.

## 5 steps that connect ads to sales

<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Tie every lead to its source.</b> The campaign and ad name must travel with the lead into the sheet or CRM, or you will never know which campaign brings deals.</li>
<li><b>Call fast.</b> Leads cool quickly. An instant alert to the sales team on every sign-up, and ads scheduled to the team's working hours, raise the answer rate.</li>
<li><b>Add a filtering question.</b> One question about company size or timing removes more unserious leads than serious ones.</li>
<li><b>Send results back to the platform.</b> Send closed leads to the platform as a separate conversion, and build lookalikes from them, not from everyone who filled the form.</li>
<li><b>Start the report with deals.</b> Closed deals, cost per deal and revenue against spend come first. Cost per lead follows as a supporting signal.</li>
</ol>
