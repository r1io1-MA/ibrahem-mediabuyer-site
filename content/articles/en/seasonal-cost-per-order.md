<p>Every store owner in Saudi Arabia has seen it: Ramadan, White Friday or National Day comes closer, cost per order jumps, and campaigns that were working well suddenly look broken. The campaigns did not break. The market they run in changed.</p>
<h2>Reason 1: the auction gets crowded</h2>
<p>Ads on Snapchat, TikTok, Meta and Google are sold by auction. In peak seasons many more brands enter with bigger budgets to reach the same people, so the cost of a thousand impressions goes up. If your conversion rate stays the same, a higher cost per impression means a higher cost per order.</p>
<h2>Reason 2: shoppers compare more</h2>
<p>During the season a shopper sees dozens of offers a day, hesitates more and compares more. They may click your ad and then buy from a competitor whose offer is clearer. A bigger budget alone is not enough; the offer itself has to be stronger.</p>
<h2>Reason 3: accounts start too late</h2>
<p>The most common mistake I see is raising the budget sharply on the first day of the season. Platforms need time to learn, and every big change to budgets or ad sets sends them back into the learning phase on the most expensive days of the year.</p>
<h2>The preparation plan: 6 steps</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Know your ceiling before you start.</b> Work out the highest cost per order your margin can carry. The <a href="/tools/break-even-roas/">break-even ROAS calculator</a> does it in a minute, and that number, not the feeling that "ads are expensive", is what you judge campaigns by in the season.</li>
<li><b>Build the audience two to three weeks early.</b> Reach and video views are cheaper before the peak. People who engage with your content before the season become a ready retargeting audience when the offer goes live.</li>
<li><b>Prepare creative early, with more than one angle.</b> Ads wear out faster in peak season because people see them more often. Prepare several angles for the offer and test them before the peak, so you enter the season with the winners.</li>
<li><b>Raise budgets gradually.</b> Instead of doubling in one day, raise it in steps over the days before, and watch cost per order after each step.</li>
<li><b>Make the offer clear in the first two seconds.</b> The discount, gift or free shipping has to show immediately, in the ad and on the product page, because shoppers compare fast.</li>
<li><b>Check tracking before anything else.</b> If purchase events are not reaching the platforms properly, the algorithm learns from wrong data on the most important days of the year. Review pixels and UTM links at least a week before the season.</li>
</ol>
<h2>A real example</h2>
<p>In <a href="/cases/edtech-251-percent-of-target/">one of my case studies</a> the month stacked two seasons, back-to-school and Saudi National Day. We did not treat it as one campaign. We split it into two jobs: the first half sold directly with an angle for parents, the second half used National Day for reach and then converted that audience with retargeting. The result was 251% of the monthly target.</p>
<div class="callout">The season is not won on the day. It is won in the weeks before it. Go in with a clear cost ceiling, a warm audience, tested ads and clean tracking.</div>
