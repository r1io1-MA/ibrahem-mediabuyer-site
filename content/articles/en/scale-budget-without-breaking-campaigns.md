Every media buyer has seen it: a campaign is doing great, the store owner doubles the budget, and the next day cost per order jumps and sales drop. The campaign usually is not the problem. **The way the budget was raised is.**

## Why campaigns break after a budget increase

- **The learning phase.** The algorithm learns from results to find buyers. A big budget change can send it back into learning, so it starts testing wider and more expensive audiences again. Meta, for example, says an ad set needs about 50 optimisation events a week to exit the learning phase.
- **The cheapest audience is used up.** The first riyals go to the easiest buyers; every extra riyal reaches people who are less ready to buy.
- **Ads wear out faster.** A bigger budget means the same ad is shown more often to the same people, and response falls.

## 6 rules for scaling safely

<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Raise in small steps.</b> Increases of around 15% to 25% every few days are safer than doubling at once. Watch cost per order after each step.</li>
<li><b>Judge against a clear ceiling.</b> Know the highest cost per order your margin can carry from the <a href="/tools/break-even-roas/">break-even ROAS calculator</a>, and keep scaling while you stay under it.</li>
<li><b>Scale sideways too.</b> Instead of pushing one campaign to its limit, copy what works into a new audience or a second platform, so you grow without squeezing the same people.</li>
<li><b>Prepare ads before you scale.</b> Scaling burns through creative faster, so go in with new angles ready to test.</li>
<li><b>Change one thing at a time.</b> Do not raise the budget, change the audience and swap the ad on the same day, or you will not know what caused any change.</li>
<li><b>Track MER, not platform ROAS alone.</b> Total store revenue ÷ total ad spend tells you whether scaling adds real sales or just reshuffles the same sales between platforms. If your store relies on cash on delivery, check the <a href="/tools/cod-roas-calculator/">real ROAS after refused orders</a>.</li>
</ol>

## A real example

In <a href="/cases/edtech-251-percent-of-target/">one of my case studies</a>, monthly TikTok spend more than doubled, from $10.5K to $22.3K, while ROAS rose from 4.47x to 4.82x and purchases doubled. It did not come from one big jump. It came from restructuring the account, testing creative continuously, and raising budget gradually on what kept working.

<div class="callout">Scaling well is not one decision. It is a series of small steps, each one measured before the next.</div>
