<p>This is one of the questions I get most from new store owners. The honest answer: there is no platform that is always better. There is a platform that fits your product, and your ability to make content, at this stage.</p>
<h2>When to start with Snapchat</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your product is an easy, mid-priced purchase.</strong> Fragrance, personal care, gifts and fashion work well on Snapchat in Saudi Arabia, because the audience is large and impulse buying is common.</li>
<li><strong>You want order volume from the start.</strong> In the <a href="/cases/ecommerce-882-orders/">perfume store case study</a> Snapchat was the volume channel: 6.63x ROAS and 591 purchases in one month.</li>
<li><strong>Your content is simple and clear.</strong> A short ad that shows the product and the offer clearly is often enough to begin.</li>
</ul>
<h2>When to start with TikTok</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Your product needs to be shown or explained.</strong> TikTok is excellent for products that convince people when they see them being used.</li>
<li><strong>You can produce a lot of fresh content.</strong> Ads on TikTok wear out faster and need new angles all the time, and creators are a core part of the plan.</li>
<li><strong>You have a season or a launch.</strong> In one account TikTok reached 8.58x ROAS in the first 3 days of the season, after the account was restructured.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to split the first budget</h2>
<p>For a new store with a limited budget, I do not recommend opening four platforms at once: the budget gets spread thin and no platform learns. Start with one main platform that takes most of the budget, and a small test on a second. After three to four weeks, move budget toward the platform with the lowest real cost per order, measured on store orders, not only on what the platform reports.</p>
<h2>What to measure in the first 30 days</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Cost per order against your ceiling.</b> Work out the most your margin can pay with the <a href="/tools/break-even-roas/">break-even ROAS calculator</a>.</li>
<li><b>MER, not platform ROAS alone.</b> Total store revenue ÷ total ad spend. This number does not lie.</li>
<li><b>Add-to-cart and conversion rate.</b> If people engage with the ad but do not buy, the problem is usually the product page, the price or the shipping, not the platform.</li>
<li><b>Ad lifespan.</b> When does an ad start to decline? That tells you how many new ads you need each week.</li>
</ol>
<div class="callout">Pick the platform that fits your product and your content capacity, concentrate the budget at first, and judge on real store orders. Expanding to other platforms comes after you find one winning channel.</div>
